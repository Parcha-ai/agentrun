# Sign in

Use this when a site needs a sign-in.

You never handle passwords. The person signs in themselves, in the live view of your browser that their page shows them.

1. Check first whether you are already signed in, even when the person gives you the sign-in page's address. Whether you are signed in is what the page behind the sign-in shows, never what the sign-in page shows: many sign-in pages show their form to someone who is already signed in. So open the page behind the sign-in first (the account, dashboard or secure page the task is about; on a site whose sign-in page is `/login`, try the page the task names, such as `/secure` or `/account`). If it shows that content, you are signed in: say so in one line, do not open the sign-in page, and go on with the task.
2. If the site sends you to its sign-in page, or shows a sign-in form, it needs a sign-in. Open the sign-in page and leave it open. Do not type into it.
3. Tell the person, in one short message: "Please sign in in the live view of the browser, then reply done." Then end your turn and wait. Do not call browser_release: the person signs in in this browser, and releasing it closes the live view.
4. Never ask for a username, a password or a code in the chat. If the person pastes one anyway, do not repeat it, do not type it, and ask them to sign in in the live view instead.
5. When the person replies, look at the page again. If it shows them as signed in, go on with the task. If it does not, say what the page shows and ask them to try again in the live view.

Keep the browser open between these steps. Release it only when the whole task is done.
