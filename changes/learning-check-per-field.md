A learned workflow's check now judges each answer field on its own, against the best known answer that field has: a label, else a stored grade's expected answer, else the agent's own answer. With the shipped bar, a field judged against the agent's answers needs at least `agree_min` (80% by default) of the inputs it answered alone to say what the agent said, and at least `agree_min` of those inputs must be right on every field at once, as before. A field judged against grades needs `agree_min` of those inputs right and at most 2 points under the agent's own accuracy on them, so a correction the agent got wrong is no free pass. With no `agree_on`, each key of a grade's expected answer is its own field (`answer.<key>`), and the whole answer must still say what the agent said. An input with no known answer (the agent's answer graded wrong with no correction, and the draft said another) is never counted as right, and a field must be judged on at least `alone_min` of its inputs. A hold names the fields that failed and the inputs each got wrong, and the revise prompt shows the known answer from a label or grade where the draft missed it, so a workflow that is right on one field and wrong on another is told which and what.
Grades are read for the first time: a grade's `expected` answer is the known answer on its fields, and a grade that says the agent's answer was wrong makes repeating it wrong. The check record gains `check.fields` (per field: inputs, right by workflow, right by agent on labelled or graded inputs, which known answer, inputs with none, pass) and `check.agent_agreed` (the agent right on every field against the same known answers), which the cost summary's `agent_pct` and a replay's `agent_acc` now report instead of a fixed 100%. Each disagreement gains `fields` and `expected`. New `learning.agree_on` paths may not hold a `__proto__` segment; saved ones still read.
