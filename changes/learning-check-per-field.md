A learned workflow's check now judges each answer field on its own, against the best known answer that field has: a label, else a stored grade's expected answer, else the agent's own answer. With the shipped bar, a field judged against the agent's answers needs at least `agree_min` (80% by default) of the inputs it answered alone to say what the agent said, and at least `agree_min` of those inputs must be right on every field at once, as before. `agree_min` applies to each field over all its known inputs pooled, wherever each one's known answer came from, so one graded input missed among 19 the agent knows passes at 19 of 20. On the inputs known from grades (and apart on those known from labels), the draft may be at most 2 points under the agent's own accuracy, so a correction the agent got wrong is no free pass. Fields come only from `agree_on`, the definition's own names. With no `agree_on` the check judges one field, the whole answer, against the agent's answer with a grade's expected keys written over it when both are JSON objects, else against the grade's expected answer whole (equal, or the same decision by the fast model's verdict on the draft and that answer), else against the agent's answer. An input with no known answer (the agent's answer graded wrong with no correction, and the draft said another) is never counted as right, and a field must be judged on at least `alone_min` of its inputs. A hold names the fields that failed and the inputs each got wrong. The revise prompt shows the known answer from a label or grade where the draft missed it, and, for an input graded wrong with no correction, that the owner marked the agent's answer wrong without giving the right one.
Grades are read for the first time: a grade's `expected` answer is the known answer (on each `agree_on` path, or written over the agent's answer), a replay of a graded run asks the fast model about the draft and that answer, and a grade that says the agent's answer was wrong makes repeating it wrong, in any dress and whatever the fast model says. A replay's verdict keeps the grade it was asked against (`replay.compared.graded`), so a grade given while the check settles counts from the next check and no longer holds a good draft as stale. A run's purge blanks the known answer copied into a check's disagreements; no grade key becomes a field name. The check record gains `check.fields` (per field: inputs, right by workflow, right by agent on labelled or graded inputs, which known answer, inputs with none, pass) and `check.agent_agreed` (the agent right on every field against the same known answers), which the cost summary's `agent_pct` and a replay's `agent_acc` now report instead of a fixed 100%. Each disagreement gains `fields` and `expected` (`label`, `grade` or `unknown`). New `learning.agree_on` paths may not hold a `__proto__` segment; saved ones still read, and an edit that leaves `learning` out carries them forward.
