A check with no `agree_on` counted a grader reply it could not read as a disagreement, so a grader that answered in prose held a draft with "its answers reached a different decision from the agent's on 3 of 3", though nothing was compared. Now a reply that is not the verdict is asked once more, as a failed request already was. A comparison that still cannot be read (two such replies, a request that failed twice, or one the run's stop or its deadline cut) is `unread`: counted neither right nor wrong, never a disagreement, and left out of the joint floor. The hold names how many were unread (`check.unread`, and the field's `unread`). With any unread comparison, a check that could compare fewer than 2 of the inputs it answered alone decides nothing: it holds with "not decided" and the counts (`check.undecided`). A recompile's check that cannot decide still stops, so a grader that never answers in the asked form cannot make every run compile. A refusal by the run's cap and a verdict on an earlier answer still count as differing. An audit of a serving workflow compares through the same grader: its row's `agreed` is now null for an unread verdict (no evidence, never counted toward `hold_disagree`), where it used to be false and counted.
