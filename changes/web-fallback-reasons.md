Every web_fetch fallback to the guarded direct fetch tells the model why. Each provider asked ahead of it (Parallel, keyed Exa, keyed Firecrawl, keyless Firecrawl) puts its reason on the page under its id, and `webFetchText` adds a line for it in the order asked: "keyless Firecrawl was picked for web_fetch and gave no page (rate-limited until 12:01 UTC); this one was fetched directly." Where no provider read the page the line says so; a page a provider behind it read carries no reason, as for Parallel. A provider held by a breaker, or one that met a limit on this read, is said by the hold: rate-limited or out of quota, until the minute it lifts (UTC), or for the rest of the run (`holdingWords`, `Reader.holding`). Parallel's other reasons and its line are unchanged; its held reason now names the hold instead of "held by its limit", and a 402 on its extract reads "out of quota for the rest of this run". Receipts and the server's log lines are unchanged: a turn a logged provider was not asked for stays off the receipt. The registry adds `AHEAD`, `providerName` and a `keyless` mark on keyless Firecrawl and keyless Exa. The direct fetch's refusal of a page that is not text is typed refused/GUARD (the guard's refusal), not not_text.
