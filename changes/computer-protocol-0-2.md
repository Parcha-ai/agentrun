packages: @parcha/agentrun-computer, @parcha/agentrun-serve
kind: breaking

The computer protocol is 0.2: a new `ui` group sees one app's window as named elements (`ui.observe`) and acts on it by short lists of steps that settle before they answer (`ui.act`), and `hello` says the screen's size and what the computer can and cannot do (`hello.screen`, `hello.features`). Breaking: the `apps.ui_tree` group and method are gone, replaced by the `ui` group; read a screen's elements with `ui.observe`, whose nodes are a flat list with refs, ARIA roles and bounds in window points, in place of the nested tree.
- `ui.act` steps: `press`, `set`, `perform`, `click`, `type`, `key`, `nav`, `scroll` and `drag`, by ref or at a point read from a state, with an optional `expect` checked on the computer. Each step answers the route that ran (`semantic`, `page`, `background`, `foreground`) and whether a readback verified it. A ref whose element is gone, or a point whose window changed under it, is refused with the new error kind `stale`, and a step on a disabled control, a type into a ref that is not a text field, or a type into a field that did not take the focus with the new kind `refused`.
- `hello.features` names what is on, or off with the reason (`ui.tree`, `ui.image`, `ui.background`, `ui.foreground`, `ui.page`, `ui.nav`, `apps.background`). With `ui.tree` off, `ui.observe` answers the window's image with no nodes and says why in `empty`. `hello.device` names the machine (`maker`, `model`), and a node's `desc` carries what else it says beside its name and value. `apps.launch` takes `url` (a deep link) and `background`.
- `@parcha/agentrun-computer/fake-ui-driver` answers `hello`, `apps` and `ui` over the wire from scripted apps (the ui fixture by default), in process (`fakeUiConnection`, `fakeUiProvider`) or on stdin and stdout. The conformance suite's new `ui` cases run on the ui fixture: `ui.hello` (Core) and, where the fixture is installed, observe's shape, refs that survive, an expect met and unmet, stale refs and points, the route each step took, a click never verified unless it landed, and each feature on doing what it says and off refused with its own sentence.
- `UiSession` calls `ui.observe` and `ui.act` with their types on one target and carries the last state to a step at a point; `findNodes` and `refusedStep` read their results.
- The attach binding is specified as a draft (`spec/computer/attach.md`): a driver dials `wss://<server>/v2/computers/attach` with a token it got by pairing a code from the console, and the same messages travel as WebSocket text frames. A driver of another protocol version, or a hello missing a field the server reads, is closed `4426` with words that say how to update it; any build within the protocol is accepted. A hello not answered in 10 s (`ATTACH_HELLO_MS`) is closed `4408`. `AttachHub` and `attachedProvider` are its server side, written against an `AttachSocket` interface; serve's routes come later. A run's ref names its hold (`<computer id>.<hold>`): its release ends only that hold, and the hold ends at the run's `lifetimeMs`.
- `UiEngine` implements the group's rules over a `UiSource`; the phone harness's Android answers `ui` through it, over uiautomator and adb's input (foreground only, unverified).
- agentrun-serve's phone tree tool reads `ui.observe`; its nodes' roles are ARIA names (`button`, `textbox`) in place of Android class names.
