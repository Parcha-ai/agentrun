The sign-in limit can no longer lock the owner out, behind a proxy or at all, and an IPv6 network counts as one address. It counted every visitor by the connection's address, so behind a proxy (Render, or the TLS proxy in front of the container) everyone was the proxy and one guesser's ten wrong passwords held the owner out, again every 15 minutes. And it counted each IPv6 address apart, so one /64 had ten tries for each of its addresses, and a full table of addresses refused every new one. Now each address (an IPv6 one by its /64) has ten tries in 15 minutes, and past them each next try waits a second, then twice that, a minute at most; the server takes 100 wrong passwords in 15 minutes before every try waits the same way. A refused try says how long (`retry-after`, `retry_after_ms`), and the console's sign-in says it. A full table no longer refuses a new address: the server's budget holds it. `AGENTRUN_TRUSTED_PROXIES` (addresses, CIDR ranges, or `loopback`, `linklocal`, `uniquelocal`) names the proxies whose `X-Forwarded-For` names the visitor; unset, the header is never read. `render.yaml` sets it to `uniquelocal`; `compose.yaml` passes it through. Adds `proxy-addr` 2.0.8 and `ipaddr.js` 2.5.0.
