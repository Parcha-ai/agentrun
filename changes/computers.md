An agent's computer is one setting, `computer: { kind, provider? }`, with kind `none`, `sandbox`, `desktop`, `phone` or `browser`. The v2 `place` stays readable and writable as its view (`{mode: "runner"}` is `{kind: "none"}`, `{mode: "sandbox", box}` is `{kind: "sandbox", provider: box}`), and writing either sets both. With no setting, Mode 1 runs Docker's sandbox where Docker is ready and none otherwise; Mode 2 runs none.

Settings → Computers lists what the server offers: each kind with its providers, their capabilities, and whether each declared key is saved, never its value. A provider's keys are saved by the names it declares, sealed in the store, and never echoed back. "Test connection" makes one account-level read that never creates a computer, and gives `ok` or the provider's words. At a run's claim the setting is checked against the server's mode and the provider's keys: Mode 1 runs a sandbox only on Docker, Mode 2 refuses Docker's sandbox, and a desktop, a phone or a browser is allowed in both. A provider with a required key missing is refused in words that name Settings → Computers. A run's read gains `computer: {kind, provider, state, why?}`.

A run's tools follow its computer's kind. A desktop adds `desktop_screen` and `desktop_input`; a phone adds `phone_screen`, `phone_tap`, `phone_swipe`, `phone_type`, `phone_apps` and `phone_open`; a browser adds pi-browser's browser tools; each comes after the file and shell tools. The run's folder is mounted in the computer only when the kind has files and exec and its provider mounts the folder: a sandbox always, and a desktop when its provider does. Otherwise bash runs in the run's folder on the runner, asking first. Screenshots come back as images. The background tools' refusal with no computer now names the setting.

A run's desktop or phone can be watched and taken over by its owner. `GET /v2/runs/{id}/computer` gives its kind, its state (`live`, `none` or `off`, with `why` when there is nothing to watch), whether the owner holds it, and the provider's live stream of its screen (view-only while the agent works). `POST /v2/runs/{id}/computer/take-over` gives the owner a stream that drives it and parks the agent's screen, input and phone calls; `POST .../hand-back` gives it back. At the hand-back a parked look runs on the screen as the owner left it, and a parked act is not done: the agent is told the person had the computer and to look again. A take-over goes back to the agent on its own after 15 minutes, or at the run's end, and the run's read says `taken_over` while it lasts. These routes are owner-only, as a run's browser view is: they take the console's password session, never a token. The browser keeps its own routes.
