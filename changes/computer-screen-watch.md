A computer's live view, as frames over the protocol (the user-approved decisions, 2026-11-09: proxy only, and every desktop's and phone's frames on one path). `screen.watch` gives the next frame after the caller's ack, once the screen changed, and `provider.watch()` is the seam the runner's viewer reads it through.
- computerd's `screen.watch({ after, waitMs?, maxWidth?, quality? })`: `after` is the seq of the last frame the caller has whole; the answer is the next frame once the screen's pixels or the pointer's place changed, else `changed: false` when `waitMs` (default 2 s, at most 10 s) is over, and an older `after` gets the newest frame at once. A frame is a JPEG scaled to `maxWidth` (default 1280) at `quality` (default 60), with its own size, the screen's size (the input space) and the cursor; one over a chunk is read on with `screen.read`. A watch reads the screen once each 200 ms at most, across calls too (an animated screen acked at once is no faster), and holds the display only for each read, so `input.actions` and `screen.shot` go on meanwhile on the same connection. A frame says `eof` false when it is over a chunk, and its rest is held apart from a shot's, so a `screen.shot` never evicts it. One watch per connection; a cancel ends it as `aborted`.
- The Android phone answers `screen.watch` itself, as PNG frames from `screencap`, read once a second at most across calls, upright as screencap gives them (no rotation of ours). A cancel or the connection's close ends a screencap and a wait in flight. Its `input.actions` batches run one at a time, and no phone error passes on the phone's endpoint: adb's connect answer gets fixed words, and the adb server's and the phone's words are redacted.
- `provider.watch?(ref, { frame(frame, ack), ended(error) }, { signal?, maxWidth?, quality? })` gives `{ input, close }`. Each frame (`{ seq, format, bytes, width, height, frameWidth, frameHeight, cursor? }`, width and height the screen's real pixels) waits for `ack()` before the next is asked for. A computer that goes ends it once with `ended(error)`, its code `gone`, `unreachable`, `refused` or `unsupported`. `close()` and the signal end it with no `ended()`, and `input()` then refuses in words. `daemonWatch` is the one implementation behind it, on Docker, Daytona, Modal, E2B, Orgo and the phone harness; a computer with no screen is `unsupported`. No error holds a URL or a secret (`redactWords`).
- `daemonWatch` adapts `watchScreen(connection, options)`, the internal seam: pull-paced `frames` (each `next()` the ack), `act()` on the same connection, and `close()`; its failures are `ScreenWatchError`s. A frame is read by the size it says (never past it, never over 64 MiB, whole at the end); 8 inputs at most are in flight on a watch (one more is `limit`); a failed action's words are redacted as errors are. `redactWords` also takes out logins, bearers, `sk-`/`gh*_` keys, JWTs and host or IPv4 endpoints.
- `toAction(event, kind)` maps the live view's `ViewerEvent`s, in the screen's pixels, to input actions (a desktop's mouse, wheel and keys; a phone's taps, swipes, buttons and keys), or to a `Refusal` naming the event's field. It checks every field at run time and never throws. A typed text holds 65,536 characters at most (`checkActions` refuses more as `limit` too, as computerd does); a swipe is clamped to 0 to 1000 ms and a wheel event to 20 clicks. A shortcut keeps Shift; a click with a modifier held is one click with its keys, on the up, and a double-click is two such clicks, never a `count`. A desktop never gets ctrl+alt+backspace or ctrl+alt+F1 to F12.
- `createActionMapper(kind)` gives `{ map(event), release() }`, one per take-over: `map` answers as `toAction` does, but a desktop's mouse press keeps its strategy to its release (a modifier let go before the up still clicks once, with the down's keys), and `release()` gives the ups for every button still pressed, then forgets them.
- Conformance: `screen.stream` (Full, a vendor's URL) is replaced by `screen.watch`, Core in the screen group: the first frame decodes (a PNG whole, a JPEG walked to its end) to the size it says, of the screen `screen.shot` reads, and a pointer move is a new frame with the cursor where it went.
