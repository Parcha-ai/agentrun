Claude Code's login on this machine, after a chmod of its folders, follow-ups to the config-folders change (#3759):

- A request that finds the login's folders changed (a run's check, a save's, capabilities) swaps the login in one turn on the sign-ins' queue and makes the offers again outside it. A sign-in, a turn-on or a turn-off asked meanwhile no longer waits for that probe of the offers; a run, save or capabilities request still waits for it.
- Where making the offers again fails after the login was swapped, the next request makes them again, rather than going on with the offers made from the login as it was.
- A template's plan and apply (`POST /v2/templates/{name}/apply`, with or without `dry_run`) now look at the login's folders first too, so the harness they pick and the model choice they check agree with a run's check right after a chmod.
- A request that finds the login as it is waits for a turn-on's or turn-off's offers still being made, and `claude_login_wait` is logged with `why` (`queue`, `refresh` or `turn`) for each wait. Once its wait ends, it goes on only if no later turn-on or turn-off started meanwhile and the offers it waited for were made. Where a later one started (its probe drops the earlier one's), it waits for that one too; where another request's making of the offers failed, it makes them again itself. A template's apply asked as a turn-on lands no longer picks its harness by the offers made before both. After 3 such waits a request is refused as `unavailable` (503, retryable), in words that say to ask again.
- A login folder its group may write in is allowed where that group is the user's private one, as the local account files alone say: `/etc/nsswitch.conf` takes users and groups from those files only (`files` or `compat` on its `passwd` and `group` lines, and on `initgroups` where it has one), and the group is the user's primary group, which no other user has as theirs and `/etc/group` lists with no other member. A umask of 002 under such a group no longer refuses the login. Where the switch also asks another source (sssd, LDAP, NIS, winbind, or systemd's user records, as the `files systemd` that recent Ubuntu, Debian and Fedora installs have), or there is no `/etc/nsswitch.conf`, the group can have members the files do not show, so the folder is refused as before. So are a group another user is in, one that is not the user's primary group, one `/etc/group` lacks, account files with a `+` or `-` line, and a folder anyone may write in, all in the same words.
