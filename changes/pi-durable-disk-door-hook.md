`AppOptions.door` hands `serve`'s door to a harness of the app's own (`DoorHook`: `submit`, `abort`, `busy`). Each submit is admitted in the root conversation (a `door.input` entry, through the run's fenced commit) before the hook sees it, a requestId reaches `hook.submit` at most once across incarnations, and its outcome settles the same record: `ADMITTED` 202 (only while the hook runs in this instance), `ACCEPTED` 200, `REJECTED_BUSY` 409, `FAILED` 500, and `UNKNOWN` 504 at the submit bound (30 s, `ServeOptions.doorTimeouts`), whose late outcome is still written. A record admitted by an instance that died or lost its lease before the outcome is `UNKNOWN`, answered so at once and written so at the next open. `POST /abort` and `GET /status` go through the hook, `GET /events` merges its `run_start` and `run_end` with the conversation's entries, and when the instance ends (a SIGTERM drain, a fence, a park) the door aborts the hook and kills the harness children it adopted. The hook emits `run_end` when its turn ends in a way the door cannot see; the door pairs `run_start` at the points it controls. Without `door` the door is unchanged.
A fenced lease now refuses every later commit through the run's storage, whatever fenced it; before, only the watchdog's lapse did.
