The console says a schedule's time once, in your own time: a weekly schedule at 08:00 UTC seen from New York reads "At 3:00 AM your time (08:00 UTC), only on Monday · next Mon Nov 16, 3:00 AM EST" on the Triggers list, the trigger's page and a template's Apply result, where it read "At 08:00, only on Monday · UTC · next Nov 16, 3:00 AM" (two clocks, the second with no zone). The schedule's own time and zone are in parentheses only when its zone is not yours; the next run carries its day and your zone's name, and both clocks read the server's next run, so a time a clock change skips or repeats reads as the hour it runs at. A cron line with more than one time ("Every 15 minutes") keeps its words, its zone said once. A one-time schedule set in the past says the time you picked, in your own time ("Pick a time in the future: 9:00 AM today has already passed."), not the API's ISO time, also when your computer's clock runs behind the server's. A template's Apply form lists each of a parameter's choices once, its default marked on its own entry ("none (default)"), and labels a schedule's cron parameter "Schedule" and its zone "Time zone", without the cron line's or the zone's raw format as help. The schedule form says itself in plain words: its zone's hint is "The time zone this schedule runs in.", the typed-line option is "Custom schedule", an empty schedule asks "Pick when it runs.", and the first-trigger panel's Schedule card no longer says "cron line".
