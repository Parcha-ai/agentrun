A process keeps running across calls and connections: `process.start` (argv, with an optional pty and stdin), `process.read` by byte offset with an optional bounded wait, `process.write`, `process.signal` (`TERM`, `INT`, `KILL`, `HUP` to the program's group), `process.resize`, `process.list` and `process.wait`. Hello's capabilities gain `processes`.
- `computerd init` now serves the protocol on a unix socket (`/run/agentrun/computerd.sock`, socket 0600) with one process table for every connection, and `computerd stdio` is a bridge to it that fails in words when no daemon listens. `DockerProvider.connect()` is unchanged. Init needs the socket's directory there already, the daemon's user's alone (0700) and writable, and exits 1 in words otherwise; `DockerProvider.create()` mounts `/run/agentrun` as a tmpfs owned by the computer's uid:gid, mode 0700, `noexec,nosuid,nodev`.
- Each stream keeps its last 8 MiB; a read below the ring's start reports the bytes `dropped`. All rings together hold at most 256 MiB (`PROCESS_RETAINED_BYTES`): past it, the rings of the processes that ended first are let go, never a running one's, and a read of one reports its bytes `dropped`, with the process still listed and its exit status kept. The table holds 256 processes; the one that ended first is dropped for a new start once its program runs (a refused start drops nothing), and a start is refused as `limit` when all 256 run.
- Writes to one process take turns; a write's 10 s bound and its cancel cover its wait for its turn.
- Every method names a signal the same way, without `SIG` (`"TERM"`, `"KILL"`): `exec.run`, `process.wait` and `process.list`.
- `process.list {from?}` answers `{processes, truncated, next}` in pages of at most 768 KiB, paged by start order (list again `from: next`), so a process dropped between pages skips no other; it lists at most 64 KiB of each argv (`argvClipped` when cut). `listProcesses` pages it whole.
- Init also ends an orphan whose guard died (a program can kill its own guard), with everything under it, and on SIGTERM, or at its `--deadline`, ends every process and exits 0; a guard its shutdown reaps first is reported as ended by `KILL`. `readAll` pages a stream until its process ends.
