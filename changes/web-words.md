What web_search and web_fetch tell the model is more accurate, and a fetched markdown page costs fewer tokens. A `web_fetch` objective given beside an offset past 0 or `max_chars` still finds in the whole page, and its `sift` line now says so (`offset ignored: found chunks come from the whole page, each with its offset`); with no classifier in the run the part's `sift` says the objective was not applied, where before it said nothing; and `WEB_FETCH_DESCRIPTION` now says what an objective answers (`found`, each chunk's `offset` and `relevance`, and `sift`). A `web_search` that meets a keyed provider's rate limit or quota (Parallel's, keyed Exa's or Firecrawl's 429 or 402, a hosted search's) answers the breaker's words, `search is busy; try again in N s, or answer from what you have` or `search is unavailable for the rest of this run; answer from what you have`, as every later call held by it does, where it answered the provider's bare status; the receipt's turn keeps the provider's words, and a keyless search's own words for its limit (`words.rateLimited`) are unchanged. A page read as markdown (Parallel, Firecrawl, Exa) no longer repeats in `links` the links its returned part already writes in `text`: `links` lists the rest, within the same caps, and `links_omitted` counts those of them the caps left out. A page fetched directly lists its links as before.
