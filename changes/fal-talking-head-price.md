A run with a spend cap can send fal's talking heads, `veed/fabric-1.0` at `"resolution": "480p"` and `fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman/v1.5`: each job is held at its voice's length, read before the call from the audio at its `audio_url` alone (on fal's own media, `fal.media` or a subdomain: a voice fal made, or one uploaded to fal), in whole seconds as fal bills them, at get_pricing's rate. The length is what the voice plays: every audio frame of an MP3 (a first frame holding a Xing, Info or VBRI header is skipped, as a decoder skips it), or a PCM WAV's samples at its sample rate, every byte after its data header counted. It is never read from the job's image, from a length its input states, or from a length the file's own header states (an MP3's Xing, Info or VBRI frame count, a WAV's byte rate or data size), which whoever made the file could write short. Only a voice on fal's own media is priced because fal fetches the `audio_url` again after this read, and another host could serve fal a longer voice than the one measured. The voice is read https only, redirects included, through the same public-host guard as web_fetch and web_save, and at most 32 MiB. A job is still refused unsent under a cap, as before, when its voice can't be read (an `audio_url` that is not https or not on `fal.media`, a private host, over 32 MiB, a timeout, a WAV that is not integer PCM or float with one format chunk whose fields agree, an MP3 with bytes that are not frames, or any other format: an mp4 or m4a voice states its length only in its header), and for Fabric at 720p or with no `resolution`: 720p costs more than the 480p rate get_pricing states, and Fabric's resolution has no default, so send `"480p"`. With no cap such a job runs, booked as an unknown charge, never $0. Fabric's reply states no length, so it is booked at its hold: the ledger books the voice's length, read before the call, not a length fal reports. OmniHuman settles at the length its reply states, in whole seconds. LatentSync, infinitalk and `ffmpeg-api/merge-audio-video` stay unpriceable under a cap.
`publicFetch` and `publicDownload` take `httpsOnly`, which refuses http on the first hop and at every redirect.
