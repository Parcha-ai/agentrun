A run with a spend cap can send fal's talking heads, `veed/fabric-1.0` at `"resolution": "480p"` and `fal-ai/bytedance/omnihuman/v1.5`: each job is held at its voice's length, read before the call from the audio at its `audio_url` alone (on fal's own media, `fal.media` or a subdomain: a voice fal made, or one uploaded to fal; the URL written exactly as it parses, so `V3.fal.media`, a space, a `..` segment or `:443` is not priced), in whole seconds as fal bills them, at get_pricing's rate. The length is what the voice plays: every audio frame of an MP3 (a first frame holding a Xing, Info or VBRI header is skipped, as a decoder skips it), or a PCM WAV's samples at its sample rate, every byte after its data header counted. It is never read from the job's image, from a length its input states, or from a length the file's own header states (an MP3's Xing, Info or VBRI frame count, a WAV's byte rate or data size), which whoever made the file could write short. Only a voice on fal's own media is priced because fal fetches the `audio_url` again after this read, and another host could serve fal a longer voice than the one measured. The voice is read https only and following no redirect (fal.media serves its files directly), so a fal.media URL can't send the read to another host, through the same public-host guard as web_fetch and web_save, and at most 32 MiB. A job is still refused unsent under a cap, as before, when its voice can't be read (an `audio_url` that is not https or not on `fal.media`, a private host, over 32 MiB, a timeout, a WAV that is not integer PCM or float with one format chunk whose fields agree, an MP3 with bytes that are not frames, or any other format: an mp4 or m4a voice states its length only in its header), and for Fabric at 720p or with no `resolution`: 720p costs more than the 480p rate get_pricing states, and Fabric's resolution has no default, so send `"480p"`. With no cap such a job runs, booked as an unknown charge, never $0. Fabric's reply states no length, so it is booked at its hold: the ledger books the voice's length, read before the call, not a length fal reports. OmniHuman settles at the length its reply states, in whole seconds. LatentSync, infinitalk and `ffmpeg-api/merge-audio-video` stay unpriceable under a cap.
`publicFetch` and `publicDownload` take `httpsOnly`, which refuses http on the first hop and at every redirect, and `maxRedirects` (a whole number from 0 to 5, default 5; with 0 any redirect is refused before its target is dialled; any other value is a `RangeError` before a lookup).
A job billed by length is priced from a length its input names only on the endpoints whose input sets the length fal bills, and only from that field: `duration` on Kling v2.5 turbo pro (text and image to video), Kling v3 pro image to video, Hailuo-02 standard text to video, Veo 3 fast, Veo 3.1 (fast, image to video) and ACE-Step; `seconds_total` on Stable Audio 2.5; `music_length_ms` or `composition_plan` on ElevenLabs Music. Before, any per-second or per-minute endpoint trusted a named `duration`, `duration_seconds`, `seconds_total` or `music_length_ms`, so a capped run could send `fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v2` with a 10 minute video and `duration: 0.001` and be held at about $0. Now dubbing, voice isolation, speech-to-text and sync-lipsync v2 are always priced from the media they are given, and any other endpoint billed by length (LatentSync, infinitalk, a merge, Fabric fast, OmniHuman v1, an endpoint this server doesn't know) is refused unsent under a cap, whatever length its input names. A listed endpoint that names no length, or another of the length fields, is refused unsent too.
