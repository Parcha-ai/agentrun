A desktop's or a phone's screen is watched and taken over live, as the browser's is. The runner opens its view as it claims the run's computer: `openComputerView` (computer-live.ts) starts the computer's viewer on loopback and hands it over in the store under the run's claim (`computer_viewers`, the token sealed); a stale claim is refused `fenced`, and an open that fails is refused `failed` in words with nothing left listening. Its WebSocket speaks the browser viewer's protocol: hello, ready with Take over, the screen's frames (JPEG or PNG) each sent once the viewer acked the one before (a frame held back for a slow viewer is replaced by a newer one, and the replaced frame is acked), and Take over and Hand back through the run's hold, the same take-over and hand-back the routes write. Input is mapped by one `createActionMapper` per take and reaches the computer only while the person holds it, as the run's own hold says at that moment, so input stops as soon as a take ends; otherwise it is answered `held` ("Take over to drive."), and what a desktop refuses (ctrl+alt+F1, ctrl+alt+Backspace) comes back in words. The mouse buttons a take holds are let go when the viewer loses focus, before a hand back, and once when a take ends on the run's side or the viewer goes. One viewer per computer; a viewer that goes mid-take hands the take back (`away`). `GET /v2/runs/{id}/computer` answers live with `kind` `desktop` or `phone` and who drives, and the viewer's own page (Take over and Hand back; a phone's back, home and recents) is served under `/v2/runs/{id}/computer/live/`, its socket proxied to the runner's endpoint with the same checks as the browser's. `@parcha/pi-browser/live-view` exports its WebSocket server and token helpers for it.
