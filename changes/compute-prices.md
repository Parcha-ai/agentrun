Computer time is priced the way providers bill it: by the resources a computer holds, never by a size. An operator copies its provider's per-resource rates from the provider's price sheet into `AGENTRUN_COMPUTE_USD_PER_VCPU_SECOND_<PROVIDER>`, `AGENTRUN_COMPUTE_USD_PER_GIB_SECOND_<PROVIDER>`, `AGENTRUN_COMPUTE_USD_PER_GPU_SECOND_<PROVIDER>_<GPU_TYPE>` and `AGENTRUN_COMPUTE_USD_PER_DISK_GB_MONTH_<PROVIDER>` (a month of 730 hours). A priced computer's time is held on the run's ledger under its `max_cost_usd`, one 5-minute stretch ahead, as browser time is: a cap with no room for the first stretch makes no computer and fails the run in the cap's words, and a cap with no room for a later stretch pauses the run for a raise; the booked time is in the run's `cost.usd`. With no price set nothing changes: the computer runs outside the cap, as a tool call with no price does, and is never refused. `GET /v2/runs/{run_id}/usage` gives `compute_time` lines (`runusage/4`), one per provider and resource, with the raw quantity, its unit, its basis and the dollars the run's ledger booked, so `total` stays the run's `cost.usd`; the time no claim held (a computer kept for a follow-up, one deleted after its run, one whose delete failed) is said apart as `unheld_quantity` and is in no cost. Each computer interval gives `disk_gb` and `booked_seconds` (Postgres migration `0023_computer_interval_resources.sql`; a SQLite store gets both columns at the server's open). A computer's figures are its provider's own where its answer gives them: `CreatedComputer.resources` in `@parcha/agentrun-computer`, filled from Daytona's sandbox (vCPUs, memory, disk and GPUs), E2B's `getInfo` and Fly's machine guest (vCPUs and memory), and `Machine.resources` for a hosted provider's read of its machine.
