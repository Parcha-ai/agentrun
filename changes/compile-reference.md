The compile turn's worked example is now the cascade a repeated job compiles to, and two checks hold drafts to it. The example fetches a page with a call step, has a fast-tier extract step copy compact facts from it, answers on the agent's model from those facts and its own draft alone, re-picks once in a loop of 2 until its quotes pass `quote_missing`, then hands back a second miss or a case the policy does not cover at an escalate step. It carries no guard.
- DSL: a whole-string placeholder marked optional, `"{draft?}"`, is null while its path is absent and the path's value once present. A re-pick loop's answer step reads its failed draft that way through its `state` map: null on the first pass, the draft on the second. An unmarked placeholder still fails with `missing_interpolation`, and `{x?}` inside a longer string stays literal text. The Lean model and the conformance corpus cover it. `placeholderValue(state, path)` is exported: the lookup every placeholder reads through (the path as written, then the path after `state.`), for a host that checks what a step reads. So is `placeholdersOf(text)`: the placeholders a string holds as interpolation fills them (a marked one only as the whole string).
- Compact state: a learned workflow draft is refused when a model step off the fast tier reads a page a call step fetched, through its `state` map or, with no map, the whole state. One refusal line names the step and what to give it.
- The quote check's re-pick now asks for that `state` map (the compact facts and `"draft": "{answer?}"`), where it asked for no map at all, which read every page on the agent's model.
- Upgrading: the compile turn asks for no step guard and refuses a draft with one (`guard`, its threshold and signal). Learned workflows already saved keep running as they are; a revision of one drops its guards.
