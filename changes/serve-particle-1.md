OpenRouter's `particle-ai/particle-1` runs, is priced and can be capped: serve lists it beside pi-ai's catalog, with pi-ai 1.5.0's entry (1,048,576-token context, 131,072 output) at Particle's own list price, $0.25 a million input tokens, $0.03 cached input and $1.00 output, until the pi family moves to pi-ai 1.5. While OpenRouter's promotional price ($0.05, $0.003, $0.50) lasts, a run's reported cost on it over-states what OpenRouter bills: on pi-ai 1.1.0 the cost is computed from the catalog price, until the pi family reports the provider's billing (pi-ai 1.2 and later do; the family's move is to 1.5). A cap errs safe this way. A caller that records the cost from OpenRouter's own usage keeps the true figure.
