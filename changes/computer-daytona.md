`DaytonaProvider`, the second computer provider, over Daytona's v0 REST API with `fetch` and no new dependency. A Daytona snapshot is the computer's image. `create` makes the sandbox with a TTL of the lifetime (`lifetime: "vendor"`), no idle stop and the network declared (`none`, `open`, or an allowlist of up to 100 domains on ports 80 and 443, or 10 IPv4 CIDRs on any port, never both), pushes `computerd` in through the toolbox's file upload, installs it root-owned, and starts `computerd init` as a session command dropped to 1000:1000 with no capabilities and `no_new_privs`. `connect` runs `computerd stdio` in a session of its own, its input through the session's input calls and its output through the command's log stream. The drift check reads the sandbox back (snapshot, network, labels, idle stop, auto-delete, TTL, class, public, KVM, GPU, volumes, link) and the daemon (hello, `/proc/self/status`, its directory and binary, the installed sha256); a mismatch deletes the sandbox at create and is refused at attach. `dist/conformance/daytona.js` runs the conformance suite on Daytona with settings read from the environment by name (`COMPUTER_DAYTONA_API_KEY`, `COMPUTER_DAYTONA_API_URL` and the rest of `COMPUTER_DAYTONA_*`; never `DAYTONA_API_KEY`, which may hold another organization's key); its live test runs only with `AGENTRUN_COMPUTER_LIVE_DAYTONA=1` and `COMPUTER_DAYTONA_API_KEY`. `ComputerCapabilities` gains `allow`, what an allowlist holds and the ports each kind of entry opens, and results a `daytona` error code. Nothing has run against real Daytona yet.
