# Low effort: one wide pass, then write

The reader wants a sharp, sourced answer now, not an exhaustive study. Low effort is one pass: search wide once, read
what came back, write once, finish.

## Your tools

The tools you have vary by run: use only the ones in your tool list. Here "search" means your web search tool (or the
browser, if that is what you have), "fetch" means your page-fetch tool (or the browser), and read, write and edit work
on files in your workspace. If a step needs a kind of tool you lack, use the closest one you have or skip the step.
Never call a tool that is not in your list. When a call fails, read its error before you retry or switch tools, and
never repeat a failing call unchanged.

## The work

1. **Cast one wide batch.** Name the parts the brief asks for. In one turn, issue the searches for every part together,
   one or two queries each. When the brief has a time window, put the dates in the queries and use a search tool's date
   filter where it has one.
2. **Read what came back.** A result's title and snippet tell you what to fetch. Fetch a page only when you need its
   text to support a claim and the result does not already say it. Take everything you need from a page (names,
   figures, dates, quotes) in one read, not a series of single-question reads.
3. **Write the deliverable once.** Right before you write, load the run's writer skill if the run has one (the skill
   for its output type, such as report-writer; your run's instructions name it) and follow it: it owns the format and
   the file name. Write that file in one pass. A run with no output file answers in its final message instead.
4. **Finish** with a short final message: what you wrote and where, and any part of the brief you could not source.

## When to stop

- **Stop searching once each part of the brief has sourced items** (inside its time window, if it has one). That is
  the signal to write. Do not run one more batch to be thorough, and do not search again to double-check what the first
  batch already answered.
- **A date a search result states counts.** Do not fetch a page only to confirm its date.
- **Follow up only for one specific missing fact** the answer depends on, with one sharpened query or one fetch, and
  only when that part of the brief would otherwise go unanswered.
- **If one pass cannot do the question justice, say so** at the end, in one line naming what is missing (a full
  chronology, a side-by-side benchmark, primary-source verification) and suggesting a run at higher effort. Then stop.
  Do not pad with weak sources to fill space.

## Sources and citations

- **Cite a page you fetched, or a search result, for exactly what it says.** A result's title, snippet, URL and date
  are citable for what they state verbatim, and nothing they only imply.
- **A link is a lead, not a source**, when it sits inside a page or inside another model's answer. Fetch it and cite
  what it says, or leave it out.
- **Every specific number, date, name and quote has a source you can cite.** Drop what no source states; never fill
  it in from memory.
- Cite inline right after the claim it supports, as `[the claim](url)`, and end a written report with a `## Sources`
  list, one `- [Title](url)` per line.

## Style

- **Lead with the answer.** The first sentence answers the question. No "this report examines" openings.
- **Structure by what the question needs**: a few sections, one per dimension that matters, no filler "Background" or
  "Conclusion".
- **Use a table** for a comparison of a few columns.
- **Length follows the question.** A single fact needs a few tight paragraphs; a comparison or a news roundup needs
  more. Cut padding, never substance.
- **If the question rests on a wrong assumption**, say so briefly rather than answer the wrong question at length.

## What low effort leaves out

- No planning file, no per-topic notes, no review loop.
- No exhaustive treatment: when the question needs one, say so (above).
- Keep the workspace root for the deliverable; put a table or list worth keeping under `data/`.

## Before you finish

- Every part the brief asks for is answered, or you say which part found nothing.
- Every item is inside the brief's time window, going by the dates its sources state, or it is dropped.
- Every specific figure, date, name and quote has the source it came from, or it is dropped.
